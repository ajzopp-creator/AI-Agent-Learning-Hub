## WHY

WO-P300-E5.006 steps 3-4 (2026-08-29, read-only, PEH-verified, pre-registered
buckets) tagged all 44,399 catalog patterns with VP-reconstructed `avg_posture`
at anchor_date, bucketed by P_010's own `determine_risk_mode` cuts, and
measured realized forward outcomes of walk-forward BUY signals:

```
regime  n_BUY   win%   expectancy  median   p10      (raw forward return, no stop, no sizing)
OFF     9,491   72.8   +6.12%      +5.15%   -6.87%
HALF    1,310   73.6   +5.99%      +4.61%   -6.60%
FULL    7,738   66.9   +5.24%      +3.72%   -7.28%
```

OFF >= FULL on BUY win rate in 5 of 6 calendar years (2026 YTD the one
reversal) and on every magnitude measure. P_010's OFF mode currently halves
risk/trade ($470 -> $235) and max position ($1,567 -> $784) for every project
in "Applies To", including P_300 -- i.e. exposure is cut in the regime where
P_300 BUY expectancy is highest.

**Counter-reading, same data:** the all-pattern base rate also rises in OFF
(59.5% vs 50.3% in FULL). BUY lift over its own regime base rate is HIGHER in
FULL (e.g. 2025: +18.5pp vs +14.6pp). The matcher is not the issue; this is
purely a sizing-policy question.

## WHAT THIS WO IS NOT

- Not a P_300 change. Not a matcher/schema change. Not a change to
  `determine_risk_mode` cuts.
- Not authorization to alter sizing. A +0.88pp per-signal expectancy edge does
  not by itself answer a portfolio-drawdown policy. The measured returns are
  pattern-level forward returns at the chosen horizon with no stop and no
  position size -- they are not P_400 trade P&L.
- 2022 alone is 38% of the OFF BUY sample (bear year where catalog patterns
  kept working). One year carrying a large share is a real caveat.

## FINDINGS AND DECISION (2026-09-13, closed same session as recorded --
no independent review required per Tony's explicit direction: this WO
recorded a decision, no code or config was built or changed)

**Q1 -- What is OFF mode FOR?** Confirmed by Tony: portfolio-level
drawdown protection in a falling tape (original design intent), not a
per-signal expectancy filter. This was the threshold question -- if intent
had been signal-quality filtering, a strong pattern-level edge might have
justified a carve-out. It isn't, so a pattern-level edge doesn't override
it on its own.

**Q2 -- do real trade outcomes agree with the raw catalog numbers?** No,
and the disagreement compounds. Two independent checks, both against the
raw WO-P300-E5.006 finding (OFF 72.8% >= FULL 66.9% raw win rate, no stop,
no sizing):
1. Real closed P_020 trades tagged `system='P_300'`: FULL 61.5% win,
   HALF 33.3%, OFF 20.0% -- opposite ordering from the raw catalog numbers.
   n=13/3/5 closed, OFF and HALF both below the n>=10 bar (WO-P115
   Workstream E), so not statistically conclusive alone.
2. Base-rate-adjusted lift (BUY win% minus that regime's own all-pattern
   base rate) is HIGHER in FULL (+18.5pp, 2025) than in OFF (+14.6pp).
   Raw win% rises in OFF partly because the ambient base rate rises in a
   beaten-down tape (59.5% vs 50.3% in FULL) -- not because the BUY tag is
   picking better. Once that is stripped out, the signal's own
   discriminative power is not stronger in OFF.
   Both independent measures point the same direction, opposite the raw
   number that motivated this WO. New Hub-wide standard from this
   finding: `Trading_Projects_Folder_Architecture.md` "Trading Research
   Standards" section, added 2026-09-13, ref P_000_Enhancement_Backlog.md.

**Q3/Q4:** not answered -- marked not-required. Both only matter if a
carve-out is on the table; there isn't one.

**Decision: KEEP. No carve-out.** P_300 BUY signals stay under P_010's
standard OFF-mode 0.5x size cut, same as every other project in
"Applies To." Three independent reasons, none of which support loosening
it: original intent was never signal-quality filtering, real closed
trades run opposite the raw catalog finding, and base-rate-adjusted lift
runs opposite it too.

**Decided by:** Tony, 2026-09-13.
