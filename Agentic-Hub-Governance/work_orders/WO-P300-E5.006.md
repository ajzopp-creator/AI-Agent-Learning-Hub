## WHY -- the question, and what the literature actually supports

Tony's hypothesis: when Pipeline B ranks catalog patterns by DTW distance to
a live candidate, it currently ignores what the candidate's SECTOR was doing.
Two patterns with identical bar geometry -- one formed while its sector was
leading, one while its sector was being sold -- are treated as equally good
analogs. Should sector state condition the match?

**Real support exists, but at the wrong horizon.** Moskowitz & Grinblatt
(1999), "Do Industries Explain Momentum?" (J. Finance 54:4, 1249-1290) is the
canonical result: industry momentum accounts for much of the individual-stock
momentum anomaly, and individual momentum is significantly less profitable
once industry momentum is controlled for. That is the strongest published
version of this hypothesis. **The catch: their formation and holding periods
are 6-12 months. P_300's forward labels are 5/7/10/15/20 TRADING DAYS.**
Nothing in that paper claims sector state predicts a 5-day move. The horizon
gap is roughly 10-30x and is not a detail -- it is the main reason this is
filed as "measure first" rather than "build it."

**Closer to usable:** Cooper, Gutierrez & Hameed (2004), "Market States and
Momentum" (J. Finance 59:3, 1345-1365) -- mean monthly momentum profit was
+0.93% following up-markets vs. -0.37% following down-markets. Real evidence
that CONTEXT conditions outcomes. But that is MARKET state, not sector state
-- and P_010 already computes exactly that (`spy_posture`, `qqq_posture`,
`avg_posture`, `risk_mode`). The Hub's existing architectural answer is that
regime context lands at P_400 sizing, NOT inside P_300's deterministic
matcher. This WO does not propose overturning that; it proposes testing
whether the data justifies revisiting it.

**Supporting reference:** Cakici et al., "Momentum and industry growth"
(Rev. Fin. Economics, 2006) -- individual stock momentum varies almost
monotonically with industry growth quintile. Closest published analog to the
exact stratification test proposed below.

**Counterweight, taken seriously:** Bailey, Borwein, Lopez de Prado & Zhu,
"The Probability of Backtest Overfitting" -- the probability of selecting an
overfit strategy grows rapidly with the number of trials. A sector-state
feature carries at least four free parameters (which ETF, what lookback,
how many buckets, relative-to-SPY or absolute). Adding four forks to the
matcher without a pre-registered test is precisely the failure mode that
paper describes.

## THE REAL COST, specific to this codebase (not generic caution)

1. **It degrades the thing that currently works.** Conditioning the corpus on
   sector regime means the top-20 becomes "closest match WITHIN the same
   sector regime" instead of "closest match overall." That trades pattern-
   shape fidelity for context fidelity. Which carries more signal is an
   empirical question, not a design preference -- and it is answerable with
   the eval machinery already built (Stage 9 sweep + ablation + LOO
   utilities).
2. **No sector mapping exists.** The `symbols` table is `(symbol_id, ticker)`
   only. A sector map, a SPDR bar ingest path, and a new feature version
   would all be new construction.
3. **Coverage gaps are real, not hypothetical.** From the live `data/live/`
   roster checked this same session: CNSWF (Constellation Software, Canadian
   OTC) and CDPYF do not map cleanly to the 11 SPDR sectors; UEC's honest
   sector proxy is URA, which is not a SPDR fund at all. Any mapping would
   need a documented fallback for these, not a silent default.
