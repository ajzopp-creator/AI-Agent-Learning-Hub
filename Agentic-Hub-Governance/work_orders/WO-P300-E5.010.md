## WHY

Investigating what's blocking CE_GATE lambda tuning surfaced that
`ledger-fill` hadn't been run since 2026-07-03 despite `buy_ledger.db`
still being written to daily. Running it today (553 unfilled rows, 362
filled) and re-running `ledger-calibration` produced a real, large-sample
result: the h5 bucket (n=472, by far the ledger's largest) shows 75.5%
predicted win rate vs. 51.1% realized, average return -0.75%. h7/h15/h20
showed positive average returns in the same report, but at n=13/6/11 --
too thin to trust on their own.

Tony asked whether the h5 shortfall was concentrated in time (a bad
stretch) or in specific symbols, or broad. That's answered below (broad).
The broad result plus the positive-but-thin longer-horizon numbers raised
a sharper, different question: is the h5 population's poor showing a
signal-quality problem, or a **timing** artifact -- the classifier's
shortest-horizon-wins tiebreak reporting a trade's outcome before an edge
that takes longer to materialize has actually shown up?

## WHAT WAS FOUND

**1. h5 gap diagnosis (broad, not concentrated).** 400 distinct tickers
across the 472 rows; 334 fired only once. Worst single week (2026-W25,
n=38, win_rate=31.6%, avg_ret=-6.2%) accounts for 19.8% of total negative
return. Worst 5 symbols by total contribution account for 12.1% of total
negative return. Neither concentration is close to a majority -- the h5
shortfall is spread across most weeks and most tickers, not driven by an
identifiable bad stretch or a handful of bad names.

*Correction, same day:* the script's first output printed `avg_return`
values without the required x100 scaling (`*_return_pct` columns are
decimal fractions -- M-020), so the saved report initially showed
`-0.007%` instead of `-0.75%`. Caught by Tony reading the pasted PEH
output against the known calibration baseline; concentration ratios
(19.8% / 12.1%) were unaffected since they're self-normalizing. The saved
report file was corrected in place and the failure mode logged as
**M-120** (a printed "matches known value" claim is not a check unless
the code asserts it -- same shape as M-051's hardcoded success string).

**2. Trajectory check -- same rows, later horizons.**

```
horizon   n(matched)  h5_win/avg        ->win/avg         delta win/avg
h7        458         50.7% / -0.81%    54.6% / +0.04%    +3.9 / +0.85
h10       445         51.0% / -0.77%    54.2% / +0.31%    +3.1 / +1.08
h15       418         51.9% / -0.65%    52.6% / -0.24%    +0.7 / +0.41
h20       324         53.1% / -0.52%    59.0% / +1.92%    +5.9 / +2.45
```

Population shrinks per horizon (only rows old enough to have that column
filled) -- this table mixes slightly different sub-populations per row,
so the cleanest read is the complete-case panel below, not this table.

**3. Complete-case panel (n=324, all five horizons filled -- the same
exact trades, five time points, no population drift):**

```
horizon   win%     avg_ret%
h5        53.1%    -0.52%
h7        56.8%    +0.63%
h10       57.4%    +1.25%
h15       56.5%    +0.98%
h20       59.0%    +1.92%
```

n=324 is a real sample, not thin -- this morning's ledger-fill backfilled
most of this cohort. The trajectory is directionally clear (-0.52% at day
5 to +1.92% at day 20, same trades) but **not perfectly monotonic** -- h15
dips slightly below h10 on both win% and avg_ret% before h20 recovers and
extends further. Worth stating plainly rather than rounding off.

**4. Market-context check (does a rising tide explain this instead?).**
Date range of the complete-case panel is 2026-06-02 to 2026-07-30. Web
search on broad-market conditions for that window: the S&P 500 fell
~1.0% in June 2026 and was "little changed" in July 2026 overall --
NOT a broadly rising market that would lift any long position the
longer it's held. A real style rotation did occur within the window
(tech/growth sold off hard in July -- Nasdaq-100 -6.6%, SOX -20.6% --
while value/cyclical/financials/healthcare held up or gained). This
weakens "rising tide" as the explanation for the h5->h20 improvement,
but leaves a narrower, still-open question: whether this cohort's
sector mix happens to skew toward whichever side benefited from that
rotation. Not checked in this WO.

## Claude's recommendation (Tony confirmed, 2026-08-31)

Real, well-designed evidence (n=324, same trades compared to themselves,
magnitude too large to be pure noise, survives a market-drift check) that
the h5 shortfall is at least partly a **timing artifact** of the
classifier's shortest-horizon tiebreak rather than purely a pattern-
quality problem. Not proof: one 2-month window, one open sector-rotation
question, one non-monotonic wrinkle at h15. **No P_300 build from this
WO.** Recommend re-running the same complete-case check once the ledger
has aged cohorts from a materially different market window (a different
quarter, ideally a different regime per P_010's OFF/HALF/FULL posture) --
if the same h5->h20 pattern holds across two structurally different
windows, that's a real case for revisiting the tiebreak rule specifically;
if it doesn't, this window was likely idiosyncratic.
