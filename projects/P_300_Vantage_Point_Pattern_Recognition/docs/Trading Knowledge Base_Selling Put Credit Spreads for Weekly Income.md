## What Is a Put Credit Spread (Bull Put Spread)?

A put credit spread (also called a **bull put spread**) involves simultaneously:

- **Selling** a put option at a higher strike price (closer to the money)
- **Buying** a put option at a lower strike price (further out of the money)
- Both options share the same underlying and expiration date

You collect a **net premium (credit)** upfront. Maximum profit is the credit received; maximum loss is the width of the spread minus the credit collected.

## The 3-Step Weekly Strategy

### Step 1: Establish That You Are Bullish for the Next Week
*(Timestamp: 2:06)*

Before entering any trade, you must have a **directional bias confirmation**:

- Use a reliable technical indicator to determine whether the index is expected to move higher or stay flat over the next 7 days
- Only enter a put credit spread when you are **bullish or neutral** on the market
- This is **not** a blind mechanical strategy — market conditions must confirm your thesis
- **Key rule:** Skip the trade entirely in weeks where you cannot establish a bullish bias
- The quality of your indicator is the single most important factor in the strategy's long-term success

### Step 2: Pull Up an Options Chain Expiring the Next Friday (7 Days Out)
*(Timestamp: 6:51)*

- Use **weekly options expiring 7 days out** (the following Friday)
- Preferred underlying: **SPX (S&P 500 Index)**
- Why SPX?
  - **Cash-settled** — no early assignment risk
  - **Tax-advantaged** — 60/40 rule (60% long-term / 40% short-term capital gains)
  - **High liquidity** — tight bid/ask spreads
  - **European-style** — can only be exercised at expiration

### Step 3: Sell a Five-Wide Put Credit Spread
*(Timestamp: 7:00)*

- Use a **$5-wide strike width** (e.g., sell the 4400 put / buy the 4395 put)
- Place the **short put at-the-money (ATM)** or just below the current price (~50 delta)
- Maximum risk per spread = **$500** (the $5 width × 100 multiplier)
- Maximum reward = **credit received** (e.g., ~$2.00 = $200 per spread)
- Target approximately a **30–40% return on risk** per week

## Profit / Loss Scenarios at Expiration

| SPX Closes | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **Above your short strike** | Keep full credit received — **maximum profit** |
| **Between your two strikes** | Partial loss (proportional to how far ITM) |
| **Below your long strike** | Maximum loss ($500 per spread minus credit received) |

## Key Concepts to Internalize

- **Credit received = max profit.** You want the spread to expire worthless.
- **5-wide spread = defined risk.** Unlike naked puts, your downside is fully capped.
- **Weekly expiration = faster theta decay.** Time (theta) decay accelerates as expiration approaches and works in your favor as the seller.
- **ATM short strike ≈ 50% probability of profit.** This is NOT a high-probability strategy — it compensates with a higher premium collected vs. OTM spreads.
- **Indicator quality is everything.** The strategy's edge comes entirely from accurately identifying bullish vs. non-bullish weeks. Skipping losing weeks is what makes it work.

## Risk Considerations & Advanced Notes

- **Volatility (VIX) level matters.** In low-volatility environments, less premium is collected. Some traders require VIX > 15–20 before entering.
- **Gamma risk near expiration.** Delta moves much faster on expiration day. Consider closing the position before Friday close rather than holding to expiration.
- **Managing winners early.** Closing at 50% of max profit is a common risk management technique that reduces gamma exposure and frees up capital.
- **Rolling losers.** Rather than taking max loss, you can roll the spread out in time for a credit to reduce cost basis.
- **Position sizing is critical.** High weekly capital allocation leads to high risk of ruin. Diversify across indicators, timeframes, or tickers if scaling.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Put Credit Spread** | Selling a higher-strike put and buying a lower-strike put for a net credit |
| **Bull Put Spread** | Another name for a put credit spread — profits when price stays flat or rises |
| **Short Put** | The put you sell; generates the credit; higher strike |
| **Long Put** | The put you buy; defines your max risk; lower strike |
| **ATM (At-the-Money)** | Strike price closest to the current price of the underlying |
| **Delta** | Measures how much the option price moves per $1 move in the underlying (~50 delta = ATM) |
| **Theta** | Time decay; works in favor of the option seller |
| **Gamma** | Rate of change of delta; increases sharply near expiration |
| **Cash-Settled** | At expiration, settled in cash — no delivery of shares, no assignment risk |
| **European-Style** | Option can only be exercised at expiration (vs. American-style = any time) |
| **60/40 Tax Rule** | SPX options taxed as 60% long-term / 40% short-term gains regardless of holding period |
