## Core Concept: What Does "70% Probability" Mean?

A **70% probability of profit (POP)** trade means the option spread has approximately a **30 delta** on the short strike — there is a 70% statistical chance the trade expires worthless (maximum profit) and a 30% chance it moves against you.

This is a **higher-probability, lower-reward** approach compared to ATM selling. The three setups all revolve around finding high-quality contexts where this 70% baseline is further reinforced by technical or structural factors.

---

## Setup #1: Sell an OTM Credit Spread at a Key Support/Resistance Level

### The Concept
Rather than blindly selling a 30-delta spread, you anchor the short strike at or near a **meaningful support level** (for put spreads) or **resistance level** (for call spreads) on the chart. This gives the 70% statistical probability additional confluence from price structure.

### How to Execute
- Identify a clear support level on SPX (or your chosen underlying) — a price zone where the market has bounced multiple times
- Sell a **put credit spread** with the short put strike AT or just below that support level
- The support level acts as a second "defense" beyond just the statistics
- Use weekly or monthly expirations (30–45 DTE is the sweet spot for premium decay)

### Why It Works
Price is less likely to breach a well-defined support level, so your short strike has both a **statistical AND technical edge** working in its favor simultaneously.

## Setup #2: Sell a Credit Spread After a Large One-Day Move (Mean Reversion)

### The Concept
When the market makes a **sharp, large single-day move** (e.g., SPX drops 1.5–2%+ in one session), implied volatility (IV) spikes and options become significantly more expensive. This creates a premium-selling opportunity because the market statistically tends to **mean-revert** after extreme moves.

### How to Execute
- Wait for a significant single-day market drop (the bigger the drop, the better the setup)
- IV rises sharply → option premiums inflate → you collect **more credit** for the same strike distance
- Sell an OTM **put credit spread** below the current price, taking advantage of elevated IV
- After a large down day, the market often stabilizes or bounces — making your OTM puts even safer

### The Dual Edge
You are being paid **MORE premium** (due to IV spike) for a strike that is now **FURTHER from price** (since price already fell). This creates two simultaneous advantages:
1. Higher credit collected
2. Greater distance between current price and your short strike

## Setup #3: Use Technical Indicators to Confirm Directional Bias

### The Concept
Before entering any credit spread, confirm that your **technical indicator signals** agree with your directional assumption. This filters out low-conviction trades and dramatically improves your actual win rate beyond the theoretical 70%.

### How to Execute
- Use a momentum or trend indicator (MACD, moving averages, RSI, etc.) to confirm market direction
- Only sell **put credit spreads** when the indicator is bullish or neutral
- Only sell **call credit spreads** when the indicator is bearish or neutral
- When the indicator is mixed or uncertain — **skip the trade entirely**

### The Filtering Effect
A raw 30-delta spread has a 70% theoretical win rate. When you layer on a confirming technical indicator and only trade when it aligns, you improve the *actual* win rate above the theoretical baseline — potentially to 75–80%+.

## Trade Construction Reference

### Bullish Setup — Put Credit Spread
- Sell a put at ~30 delta (OTM, below current price)
- Buy a put 5–10 points lower (defines max risk)
- Collect net credit
- **Max profit** = full credit if spread expires worthless
- **Max loss** = spread width minus credit received

### Bearish Setup — Call Credit Spread
- Sell a call at ~30 delta (OTM, above current price)
- Buy a call 5–10 points higher (defines max risk)
- Same mechanics as put credit spread, opposite direction

### Neutral Setup — Iron Condor
- Combine a put credit spread + call credit spread simultaneously
- Profit if the market stays within a defined range
- Collect premium from both sides
- Wider the range = lower credit but higher probability of profit

## Risk Management Rules

- **Close winners early:** Close at 50% of max profit to reduce gamma risk and free up capital — don't hold every trade to expiration
- **Stop losses:** Close position if loss reaches 2× the credit received (e.g., collected $2.00 → close if loss hits $4.00)
- **Position sizing:** Keep any single trade to 1–5% of total account value at risk
- **Skip low-conviction setups:** If the technical setup is unclear, premium is thin, or volatility is very low — do not force the trade
- **IV Rank filter:** Prefer selling premium when IV Rank is elevated (>30–50%) — you collect more credit for the same risk

## Key Principles to Internalize

- **Probability is your edge, not prediction.** You don't need to be right 100% of the time — your win rate just needs to exceed your risk/reward ratio over many trades.
- **Three setups compound your edge.** Each setup adds a layer of confirmation on top of the 70% statistical baseline.
- **Support/resistance + statistics = higher confidence.** Anchoring your strike to a technical level means both math AND price structure work for you.
- **Mean reversion after large moves is a real statistical phenomenon.** Elevated IV after a selloff is a gift to premium sellers.
- **Indicator filtering turns 70% theoretical into potentially 75–80%+ actual** by eliminating trades taken in unfavorable market conditions.
- **Consistency beats perfection.** The goal is to apply these setups repeatedly over time, not to win every single trade.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Probability of Profit (POP)** | Statistical likelihood a trade expires profitable, based on the option's delta |
| **30 Delta** | An option with ~30% chance of expiring in-the-money; implies ~70% chance of expiring worthless |
| **OTM (Out-of-the-Money)** | A put strike below current price, or a call strike above current price |
| **IV / Implied Volatility** | Market's expectation of future price movement; higher IV = higher option premiums |
| **IV Rank** | Where current IV sits relative to its 52-week range (0–100%); higher = more expensive options |
| **Mean Reversion** | Tendency of price to return toward its average after an extreme move |
| **Support Level** | A price zone where buyers have historically stepped in, preventing further decline |
| **Resistance Level** | A price zone where sellers have historically stepped in, preventing further advance |
| **Iron Condor** | A neutral strategy combining a put credit spread and call credit spread on the same underlying |
| **DTE (Days to Expiration)** | Number of calendar days until the option expires; 30–45 DTE is a common sweet spot for sellers |
| **Theta** | Time decay; erodes option value daily — works in favor of the option seller |
| **Gamma** | Accelerates delta changes near expiration; increases risk for sellers close to expiry |
