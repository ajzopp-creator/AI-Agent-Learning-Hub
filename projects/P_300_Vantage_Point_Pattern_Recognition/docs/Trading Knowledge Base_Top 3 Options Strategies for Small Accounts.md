## Who This Is For

Traders with **small accounts** (~$1,000–$5,000) who want to trade options without needing massive capital. Each strategy features a **defined, limited maximum loss** — essential for protecting a small account from a single catastrophic trade.

## Strategy #1 — Iron Condor
*(Timestamp: 2:22)*

### What It Is
A neutral, income-generating strategy that profits when the underlying stays within a defined price range. Combines a put credit spread (below market) and a call credit spread (above market).

### Structure
- Sell an OTM put + Buy a further OTM put → **Put credit spread (bullish side)**
- Sell an OTM call + Buy a further OTM call → **Call credit spread (bearish side)**
- Net result: Collect premium from both sides

### Best Conditions
Low-volatility, sideways/range-bound markets. Works well on indexes (SPX) or broad ETFs (SPY). Avoid entering around earnings or major economic events.

### Profit / Loss
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Price stays between short strikes at expiration | **Max profit** = total credit collected |
| Price breaches one short strike | Partial loss |
| Price moves beyond one long strike | **Max loss** = spread width minus total credit |

### Small Account Advantage
- Max loss is fully defined and limited
- Can enter for a few hundred dollars of risk
- No stock ownership required

### Key Rule
Only trade on assets NOT expected to make large moves. Skip iron condors during earnings or news-heavy weeks.

## Strategy #2 — Debit Spread
*(Timestamp: 6:23)*

### What It Is
A directional strategy where you buy one option and sell another at a different strike to offset cost. Dramatically reduces capital outlay vs. buying a single option outright.

### Structure — Bull Call Debit Spread (Bullish)
- Buy an ATM or slightly ITM call
- Sell a further OTM call
- Pay a **net debit** (cost to enter the trade)

### Structure — Bear Put Debit Spread (Bearish)
- Buy an ATM or slightly ITM put
- Sell a further OTM put
- Same mechanics, opposite direction

### Best Conditions
Strong directional conviction — you need price to move in your direction to profit. Not a neutral strategy.

### Profit / Loss
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Price moves strongly in your direction | **Max profit** = spread width minus debit paid |
| Price stays flat or moves slightly | Partial loss or breakeven |
| Price moves against you | **Max loss** = debit paid (fully capped) |

**Breakeven = Long strike + debit paid** (for call spreads)

### Small Account Advantage
- A debit spread can cost $100–$300 vs. $500–$1,000+ for a single option
- Similar directional exposure at a fraction of the price
- Risk is 100% capped at premium paid — no surprises

### Key Rule
Only enter with strong directional conviction and a clear catalyst. Debit spreads need movement to profit — do not use in sideways markets.

## Strategy #3 — LEAP Options
*(Timestamp: 11:23)*

### What It Is
LEAPS (Long-term Equity AnticiPation Securities) are options with **1–3 year expirations**. Buying a deep ITM LEAP call gives stock-like exposure at a fraction of the capital required to own 100 shares.

### Structure
- Buy a **deep in-the-money (ITM) call** with 1–2 year expiration (target 70–85 delta)
- The deep ITM LEAP behaves almost identically to owning 100 shares
- Cost = typically 20–40% of what 100 shares would cost

### Best Conditions
Long-term bullish on a stock or index but lacking the capital to buy 100 shares outright. Best on high-quality, fundamentally strong companies.

### Profit / Loss
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Stock rises significantly | Near 1:1 gain with stock movement (at 80+ delta) |
| Stock stays flat | Slow loss from time decay (but minimal on deep ITM LEAPs) |
| Stock falls sharply | **Max loss** = premium paid (much less than owning 100 shares) |

### Small Account Advantage
- Buy exposure to a $200 stock for $3,000–$5,000 instead of $20,000
- Long expiration = time for thesis to play out without theta destroying value
- Fully defined risk — no margin required

### Key Rule
Always buy **deep ITM** (delta 0.70–0.85). Avoid cheap OTM LEAPs — they are lottery tickets. Choose high-conviction, high-quality companies for long-term holds.

## Key Principles for Small Account Traders

- **Always use defined-risk strategies.** Never sell naked options — one bad trade can wipe a small account.
- **Start with 1 contract at a time.** Master the mechanics before scaling up size.
- **Emotions are amplified on small accounts.** A $200 loss on a $1,000 account is 20% of capital. Size positions so losses don't affect your decision-making.
- **Don't force trades.** The best trade on a small account is sometimes no trade at all.
- **Broker approval levels matter.** Iron condors and debit spreads typically require Level 2 options approval. Naked options require Level 3–4.
- **Focus on consistency, not home runs.** Small, repeatable wins compound over time.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Iron Condor** | Neutral strategy combining a put credit spread + call credit spread; profits if price stays in range |
| **Debit Spread** | Directional spread where you pay a net debit; max loss = debit paid |
| **LEAP** | Long-term option with 1–3 year expiration (Long-term Equity AnticiPation Security) |
| **ITM (In-the-Money)** | A call with strike below current price, or a put with strike above current price |
| **OTM (Out-of-the-Money)** | A call with strike above current price, or a put with strike below current price |
| **Delta** | Measures how much the option moves per $1 in the underlying; deep ITM = 0.70–0.85 delta |
| **Defined Risk** | A trade where max loss is known and capped before entering |
| **Theta (Time Decay)** | Daily erosion of option value; hurts option buyers, helps option sellers |
| **Credit Spread** | Spread where you collect a net premium upfront; max profit = credit received |
| **Debit Spread** | Spread where you pay a net premium upfront; max profit = spread width minus debit |
