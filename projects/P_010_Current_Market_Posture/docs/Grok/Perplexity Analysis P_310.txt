I ran this through Claude Opus 5, GPT 5.6 Sol, and Gemini 3.7 Flash independently — including a Python replica Claude Opus 5 built and back-tested against real SPY/NVDA/AAPL data. All three individual reports plus a synthesis are shared above.

Where they agree — real bugs, not opinions
vwap isn't VWAP. (high+low+close)/3 is Typical Price with zero volume weighting. vwapZ measures where the close sits inside the bar range, not institutional VWAP stretch.

The OBV block is dead weight. TotalSum(x) - TotalSum(x)[1] algebraically equals x — verified true on every real bar tested. obvZ is just a redundant restatement of one-bar signed volume.

The 7 z-scores aren't 7 independent votes. PCA on real data puts effective dimensionality at ~3.2–3.5, not 7. The composite double-counts a momentum cluster and a flow cluster.

The triple ExpAverage cascade adds 3–7 bars of lag and asymmetrically crushes the fast/noisy inputs (VWAP, volume, OBV, %gain) while barely touching the already-smoothed slow ones (CMF, RSI, MACD) — so volume ends up contributing almost nothing to the "participation" signal.

Regime logic tests raw z-scores while labels show smoothed ones — they disagree in sign 17–39% of the time, so the panel can display the opposite of what's actually triggering a regime.

else if ordering permanently shadows the most extreme regimes — HELICOPTER MONEY and FIRE SALE never fire in practice; several are mathematically unreachable given the volume-metric collinearity.

±7.5 thresholds are effectively (and in Claude Opus 5's math, literally) unreachable.

chop is declared but never used, and the trailing "adaptive extreme" block is fully disconnected from everything.

Missing entirely: a volatility regime (ATR/squeeze), relative strength vs. SPY, and any Alert() calls — so the study can't notify or feed a scan.

No hysteresis — the regime label flickers roughly every 4–5 bars.

Where they diverge
Does sumZZ literally collapse to zero in a strong trend? Gemini argues yes, algebraically. Claude Opus 5 tested this on real data and didn't see a literal collapse — instead found sumZZ's correlation with forward returns is ≈0 and often backwards (on NVDA, the "bearish" BREAKDOWN CONFIRMED label preceded an 81%-win-rate bullish move).

How confident can you be this produces an edge at all? Claude Opus 5 quantified it empirically and found the shipped polarity is unvalidated and sometimes literally inverted; GPT 5.6 Sol refuses to assign any win rate until the script has real entry/exit rules and walk-forward testing; Gemini offers an unvalidated "~40–55% false-breakout reduction" estimate from fixing the bugs.

How to fix it: Claude Opus 5 favors empirically-derived thresholds from your own data; GPT 5.6 Sol favors a state-vs-event architectural split with lagged/causal z-scores; Gemini ships a ready-to-paste V2 script with a Bollinger/Keltner squeeze that finally activates chop.

My take
Do this in three stages: (1) fix the correctness bugs all three flagged — real vwap(), drop the redundant OBV block, fix the volume sign/magnitude conflation, wire up or delete chop and the dead adaptive block, stop mixing raw/smoothed values; (2) adopt GPT 5.6 Sol's state-plus-event split with hysteresis so the extreme regimes stop being shadowed; (3) before trusting any new thresholds, back-test the fixed version against your own traded symbols the way Claude Opus 5 did — its NVDA finding (bearish label = bullish signal) is the strongest argument that this needs validation, not just cleanup, before it should influence real entries under your Council framework.
