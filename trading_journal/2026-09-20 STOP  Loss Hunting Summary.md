## Part 1: What is Stop Loss Hunting?

Stop loss hunting is a market manipulation move where big players push the price to trigger retail traders’ stop losses, then reverse the price in the original direction.

It happens because a large number of stop losses are placed at obvious levels, giving liquidity to institutions.

- It’s not a real breakout. It’s a **liquidity grab** before the actual move.
- Big players love retail stop losses because they provide the liquidity to enter large positions at better prices.

**Example:** Price breaks support, triggers stop losses, takes liquidity, then moves in the real direction (uptrend).

## Part 2: Where Does Stop Loss Hunting Happen?

Stop loss hunting usually happens at obvious levels where a large number of traders place their stop losses. These are key liquidity zones for big players.

1. **Support Levels** – Many traders place stop losses just below support. Price often breaks support to trigger stops, then reverses.
2. **Resistance Levels** – Many traders place stop losses just above resistance. Price often breaks resistance to trigger stops, then reverses.
3. **Previous Highs / Lows** – Stop losses are clustered above previous highs or below previous lows. These levels act as liquidity pools.
4. **Round Numbers** – Psychological levels like 24,000, 25,000 etc. attract many stop losses. Price often spikes beyond these levels to trigger stops.

**Key Lesson:** Don’t just trade breakouts… understand where the liquidity is hiding.

## Part 3: How to Avoid Stop Loss Hunting?

Stop loss hunting is a reality, but you can protect yourself with the right approach, placement and confirmation.

1. **Don’t Place Stops at Obvious Levels**  
   Avoid keeping stop losses at exact support or resistance. Don’t place stops at round numbers. Give some buffer.

2. **Use Logical Stop Placement**  
   Place stop loss beyond liquidity zones (below recent swing low/high). Use market structure instead of random levels. Let the market breathe.

3. **Wait for Confirmation**  
   Don’t enter immediately on a breakout. Wait for 2–3 candle confirmation. Check volume and price action. Avoid emotional entries.

4. **Use Multiple Confirmation Tools**  
   Use trend, structure and higher timeframe. Look for volume confirmation. Use indicators (EMA, VWAP) as filters. Focus on overall market context.

A good trader doesn’t avoid losses… they avoid unnecessary traps.

## Part 4: Real Examples & Key Takeaways

Stop loss hunting is not a random event — it often happens around important market levels where liquidity is concentrated.

**What happens?**  
Price breaks support/resistance → Retail stop losses get triggered → Liquidity gets collected → Market reverses and moves in the real direction.

### Key Takeaways
1. **Stop Loss Hunting is Normal** – It is a common market practice, especially at key levels.
2. **Don’t Be Predictable** – Avoid placing stop losses at obvious levels (round numbers, support/resistance).
3. **Use Proper Analysis** – Rely on market structure, volume and confirmation before entering trades.
4. **Be Patient** – Let the market show its intention. Avoid emotional and impulsive trades.

**Trade with Discipline, Not Emotion.**
