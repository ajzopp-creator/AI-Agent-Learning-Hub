## Part 1: What is a Gap?

A gap is a price range on the chart where no trading takes place between two candles.

It usually happens due to strong news, earnings, major events, or a sudden shift in market sentiment.

A gap shows a **sudden imbalance** between buyers and sellers.

Understanding gaps helps traders identify market strength, potential opportunities, and possible continuation or reversal zones.

## Part 2: Types of Gaps

There are 4 main types of gaps in trading:

1. **Common Gap**  
   Occurs frequently. Usually filled quickly. Less trading opportunity. Often happens in sideways or range-bound markets.

2. **Breakaway Gap**  
   Occurs at the start of a new trend. High trading volume. Often not filled. Shows strong conviction from buyers or sellers.

3. **Continuation Gap** (also called Runaway Gap)  
   Occurs in the middle of a trend. Shows trend strength. Usually not filled. Indicates the trend is likely to continue.

4. **Exhaustion Gap**  
   Occurs near the end of a trend. High trading volume. Often gets filled. Signals weakening momentum and potential reversal.

A professional trader doesn’t just see a gap — they understand what type of gap it is and what story the market is telling.

## Part 3: Gap Trading Strategies

Different gaps require different trading approaches. Here are the 3 most common strategies:

1. **Gap and Go (Trend Continuation)**  
   Trade in the direction of the gap. Look for strong volume and momentum. Works best with breakout and continuation gaps. Enter after a small pullback or consolidation. Keep stop loss below/above the gap.

2. **Gap Fill (Reversal)**  
   Trade for the gap to be filled. Look for weakness after the gap. Common and exhaustion gaps often get filled. Enter in the opposite direction of the gap. Use price action confirmation.

3. **Gap Fade (Short-term)**  
   Trade against the gap. Useful for common gaps, especially in sideways markets. Look for quick profit opportunities. Enter near the open with tight stop loss.

A gap is only an opportunity — not a signal. Always combine market structure, volume, price action, and risk management.

## Part 4: Risk Management in Gap Trading

Gaps can create big opportunities, but they are also risky. Proper risk management helps you protect your capital.

**Key Rules:**

1. **Place the Right Stop Loss**  
   - Gap Up → Stop Loss below the gap area  
   - Gap Down → Stop Loss above the gap area  
   Never trade without a stop loss.

2. **Manage Position Size**  
   Risk only 1–2% of your capital per trade. Reduce position size during high volatility.

3. **Wait for Confirmation**  
   Don’t enter blindly at the open. Let price action + volume confirm the move.

4. **Avoid Overtrading**  
   Not every gap is tradable. Focus only on high-probability setups. Avoid revenge trading.

**Remember:** Survival comes before profits. A disciplined trader stays in the market long enough to win.
